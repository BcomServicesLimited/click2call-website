Setting up a Melbourne business phone system yourself

Click2Call is built for small businesses that would rather set things up than wait for a technician. Here's what self-setup looks like from start to finish.

  1. 1Sign up for the free trial. It runs for 7 days, includes $11 of call credit and doesn't need a card. Our team activates new accounts, which takes anywhere from a minute to 24 hours.
  2. 2Pick your 03 number. Each user plan includes a local number. You can port your existing number later, once you're happy with the system.
  3. 3Add your team. Create a user for each person who makes and takes calls, and give each one an extension.
  4. 4Choose your phones. Install the Secure VoIP app on mobiles or a softphone on laptops, or connect desk phones. Eight brands of desk phone configure themselves automatically.
  5. 5Build your call flow. Decide what happens when someone rings: ring everyone at once, try people in order, play a menu, or queue callers.
  6. 6Set your hours. Add opening hours and public holidays so after-hours calls go to voicemail, a mobile or the Answer Agent.

Every step has a guide in the help centre. If you get stuck, ring support on 1300 884 879, or hand the whole job to us with managed setup.

03 numbers for Melbourne and Victoria

Melbourne numbers start with 03, the area code covering Victoria and Tasmania. A local number is familiar to Melbourne callers, and calls to it are included: inbound calls to local numbers are unlimited on the user plan.

If you already have a number, porting it costs $100 inc GST per number and usually takes 5–10 business days. The number keeps working on your current service while the port is under way. For a 1300 or 1800 number, allow up to 30 days.

You can hold numbers from other states on the same account too. A Melbourne business with Sydney clients might add an 02 number for $10 a month that rings the same team.

How different Melbourne businesses set it up

The same system suits very different businesses. Three example setups, using real Melbourne suburbs to make them concrete:

A studio with office and remote staff

Picture a design studio off Smith Street in Fitzroy with half its team working from Northcote and Brunswick. Everyone has an extension on their laptop or mobile, the studio number rings the lot, and clients never know who's at home.

Two clinics, one number

A physio with rooms in Caulfield and Moorabbin can use a simple menu (press 1 for Caulfield, 2 for Moorabbin) and let calls overflow to the other front desk when one is busy.

A sole trader with a city desk

A consultant renting a desk in a Southbank co-working space can run the business number from their mobile, take calls between meetings and let the Answer Agent take messages during them.

None of these needs special hardware. Each runs on the standard plan, configured in the portal.

Working from co-working spaces and home offices

Plenty of Melbourne businesses work from shared offices and spare bedrooms. Shared Wi-Fi can be the weak point for internet calls, so a few checks help:

  • ✓Use a wired connection for a desk phone where you can, and prefer 5 GHz Wi-Fi for laptops and mobiles
  • ✓If calls connect but one person can't hear the other, the router's SIP ALG setting is the usual culprit, and our help guide explains the fix
  • ✓For calls on the move, forward incoming calls to your mobile number, which doesn't rely on data at all

Because the number isn't tied to an address, moving from a Richmond co-working space to your own office in Prahran doesn't affect your phones. Plug in at the new place and keep going.

Choosing your phones: app, softphone or desk phone

Each user can have more than one device, so this isn't an either-or decision. Here's how the options compare for a small business:

Secure VoIP app

Turns a mobile into a business extension over Wi-Fi or mobile data. Ideal for outgoing calls that show the business number. For incoming calls on the move, forwarding to the mobile number is more dependable.

Softphone on a laptop

A phone on your computer screen, used with a headset. Suits people who spend the day at a desk or in a co-working space and want calls alongside their work.

Desk phone

A dedicated handset that's always on and never needs charging. Eight brands set themselves up when plugged in, and Yealink models are $450 ex GST.

Cordless handset

For a shopfront, café or workshop where staff move around. Yealink cordless handsets are $400 ex GST.

Any standard SIP phone works, so check what you already own before buying. The portal configures Yealink, Grandstream, Polycom, Fanvil, Panasonic, Alcatel-Lucent, FlyingVoice and Cisco SPA phones automatically, and an ordinary analogue phone can connect through an adaptor (ATA).

Daylight saving, opening hours and support

Victoria uses daylight saving from October to April. Click2Call's support line, 1300 884 879, runs Monday to Friday from 9 am to 5 pm Queensland time, which is 10 am to 6 pm in Melbourne during daylight saving and 9 am to 5 pm for the rest of the year.

Your own opening hours live in the portal. Set them once and the system switches to your after-hours plan each evening and back each morning. Public holidays can be added by date, including Victorian ones like Melbourne Cup Day and the Friday before the AFL Grand Final, so the right message plays on the day.

Features small businesses actually use

A full phone system has dozens of settings. These are the ones small businesses tend to switch on first:

Voicemail to email

Messages arrive in your inbox as audio with a written transcript, so you can read them in a meeting.

Answer Agent

Your phone rings first. If you miss it, an AI asks the caller's name, number and reason and emails you the details. It's free.

Missed-call alerts

A text or email for every call that rings out, so you can call straight back.

Call recording

Switch it on per line, with an optional notice to callers, and get transcripts and summaries.

Business hours

Daytime and after-hours routing that switches automatically, plus public holidays by date.

AI Speech

Type a greeting and have it read in a natural voice instead of recording it yourself.
