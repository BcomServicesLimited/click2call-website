What a hosted PBX means for your business

A PBX is the switchboard that decides where calls go. A hosted PBX is that switchboard running in the cloud instead of in a box in your office. There's nothing on the wall to maintain, no technician visit, and no hardware to replace when it ages.

You manage the system in a web portal: add a user, change who answers the main number, record a new greeting or set the Christmas shutdown. Phones connect over your internet connection, and the same system works whether your staff are in the office at Varsity Lakes, at home in Helensvale or out on a job in Nerang.

The Gold Coast calendar has its peaks: school holidays, summer, Schoolies, major events and long weekends. For tourism, hospitality, retail and the trades that serve them, that means bursts of calls at exactly the time staff are busiest.

A few settings make the peaks easier:

Across the border: Tweed Heads and Coolangatta

The Gold Coast has a time-zone quirk most cities don't. Coolangatta in Queensland and Tweed Heads in New South Wales are twin towns divided by the border. From October to April, New South Wales runs on daylight saving and Queensland doesn't, so the two sides are an hour apart for half the year.

For businesses with staff, customers or premises on both sides, a cloud phone system keeps things simple. Everyone shares one system, extensions dial each other directly, calls can ring staff on either side of the border, and opening hours are set once in the portal. Say which time you mean in your greeting, and callers on both sides know when you're open.

Storms, outages and staying reachable

Summer storms on the coast can take out power and internet without warning. Turn on Forward when Offline for your main number and, if your phones drop offline, calls are sent to a mobile instead. Voicemail keeps running in the cloud, so messages still reach your email.

A ring group that includes a mobile alongside the desk phones gives you a second path, so calls reach someone whether or not the office is online.

Replacing an older phone system

If your office still has a phone system box on the wall, installed years ago and understood by one person, moving to a hosted PBX is mostly a matter of doing things in the right order.

  1. 1List what the old system does today: which numbers you have, who answers what, and what callers hear after hours.
  2. 2Rebuild that in the Click2Call portal during the free trial, using the trial number.
  3. 3Check your handsets. Any standard SIP desk phone can be reused. Proprietary handsets from older systems can't, but ordinary analogue phones can connect through an adaptor (ATA).
  4. 4Port your numbers and, once they've arrived, switch off the old box.

Look out for anything else that uses your phone line, such as EFTPOS terminals or lift phones, and check with each supplier before the old line is cancelled. Monitored alarm systems aren't recommended over any internet phone service, so talk to your alarm company about another connection.
