What Is a Cloud PBX Phone System?

A cloud PBX is a business phone system where the switchboard — the PBX, or Private Branch Exchange — runs on your provider’s servers and is delivered over the internet, rather than sitting in a comms cabinet at your office. You manage call routing, menus, voicemail and recording from a web portal. The same product is also called a hosted PBX, a virtual PBX or a cloud phone system; the terms are interchangeable.

Calls travel as data over your existing internet connection using VoIP, so there are no dedicated phone lines to rent and no hardware to buy. Adding a user takes seconds, a change to your call routing takes effect immediately, and staff can answer the business number from a desk phone, a computer or their mobile, wherever they are.

Click2Call is an Australian cloud PBX provider. Numbers are Australian, support is Australian, and call recordings, transcripts and AI outputs are stored in Australia and New Zealand. Whether you are a sole trader who wants a professional business number or a multi-site business that needs one call flow across every location, the system is the same — and so is the price.

Cloud PBX vs VoIP: What’s the Difference?

They are not competing products. VoIP is the technology that carries a call over the internet. A cloud PBX is the phone system built on top of it. Asking whether to choose VoIP or a cloud PBX is like asking whether to choose electricity or a fridge.

VoIP — Voice over Internet Protocol — means voice travels as data over your internet connection instead of a copper line. A plain VoIP service gives you a number and a line, and not much else.

A cloud PBX is the switchboard. It decides what happens once a call arrives: which phones ring and in what order, what happens after hours, whether the caller hears a menu, where voicemail goes, whether the call is recorded and transcribed. It runs on VoIP, but the value is in the routing and the features rather than the carriage.

The distinction matters when comparing quotes. A cheap “VoIP plan” and a cloud PBX at a similar price are not the same purchase — one is a line, the other is a phone system. The one case where plain carriage is the right buy is when you already own a PBX you are happy with and only want to replace the lines feeding it. That is what SIP trunks are for.
