Call Queues

A queue holds callers while your team is busy, instead of letting the phone ring out or handing back an engaged tone. It is switched on per phone line, so your main number can queue while a direct line does not.

Who rings, and in what order

You add your team to the queue as a numbered list of agents and choose how they are tried. Ring All rings everyone simultaneously, so whoever is free first picks up — the usual choice for a small team. You set how long each agent's phone rings before the queue moves on, how long to wait before trying someone again, and a wrap-up period so nobody gets a new call the instant they finish the last one.

You can also decide whether the queue should ring someone who is already on a call, and whether staff can see waiting calls on their handset's busy-lamp keys and pick them up directly.

What the caller hears

Callers hear your own music on hold and your own queue announcement, both uploaded in the portal. You choose how often they are told their position in the queue, whether that announcement includes an estimated hold time, and how often a short "thank you for holding" message plays in between. Any of it can be switched off.

Limits and what happens at the end

Set a maximum number of callers allowed to wait, or leave it unlimited. Set a maximum wait time, then decide what happens when it is reached — voicemail, an overflow to another team, or a different number entirely. A queue identifier can be shown in the caller ID so staff know a call arrived from the queue rather than direct.

Queues run on a time schedule, so the same number can queue during trading hours and route somewhere else outside them.

A practical note. A queue is not always the right answer. If callers routinely wait more than a couple of minutes, a queue only makes the wait more orderly — it does not fix being understaffed on the phones. Pairing a queue with the AI Receptionist works better for most businesses, because the AI can answer the straightforward questions outright and only queue the callers who genuinely need a person.

The Live Call Dashboard

Live calls, queue activity and team availability in one place, updating continuously. It answers the question every manager asks at 11am: is the phone busy right now, and is anyone waiting?

Today's numbers, live

Counters across the top show total calls, incoming, outgoing, internal and missed, alongside total talk time and average talk time. Filter by period and by activity type. Missed calls are the number most businesses have never actually seen before, and it is usually the one that prompts a change.

Calls in progress and callers waiting

The active calls table lists each call underway — whether it is inbound, outbound or internal, who is on it, when it connected and how long it has been running. Below it, the queued calls table shows anyone waiting: which queue they are in, how long they have been holding and what state the call is in. A team status panel shows who is available.

Put it on the wall

A full-screen TV view turns the dashboard into a wallboard for a monitor on the office floor, so the team can see a queue building without anyone opening the portal. This is the piece most often sold as a separate supervisor or analytics licence elsewhere.

Recording, Transcription and Sentiment

The dashboard tells you what is happening now. This is what tells you what happened.

Every call is recorded automatically, transcribed by AI and summarised in a couple of plain-English sentences, then scored for sentiment — whether the caller sounded satisfied, neutral or frustrated. You read a summary instead of listening to a recording, which is the difference between reviewing calls occasionally and reviewing them properly.

Recordings, transcripts, summaries and sentiment data are stored on servers in Australia and New Zealand and are never sent offshore. Recording can be enabled or disabled per number. Call recording law differs between Australian states, so notify callers in line with the rules where you operate.

All of it is available through the API and webhooks, so call outcomes can be written into a CRM automatically. More detail on the analysis tools is on the AI voice tools page.

You Do Not Need to Be a Call Centre

Most businesses using these features would never describe themselves as one. If more calls sometimes arrive than you have people free to answer, you have the problem a queue solves.

Retail and multi-store businesses. Saturday morning, three customers ringing about deliveries and one person on the floor. A queue keeps them, the dashboard shows the manager it is happening.

Medical, dental and allied health. The phone rings while reception is with a patient at the desk. See our practice phone systems page.

Trades and service businesses. Everyone is on a job. Calls queue briefly, then overflow to whoever is closest to a phone rather than going to voicemail. See phone systems for tradies.

Property and professional services. Enquiries arrive in bursts after a listing goes live or a bill run goes out. The missed-call counter shows what those bursts actually cost.

How many agents can I put in a call queue?
