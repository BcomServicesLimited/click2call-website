Cloud PBX vs Traditional PBX: What’s the Difference?

A traditional PBX is a phone system in your office; a cloud PBX runs in a provider’s data centre. Costs, outages, call quality, switching and which to choose in Australia.

Reviewed by Royce Clark, Director · Last checked 27 November 2026

By Royce Clark • Updated 27 Nov 2026

A traditional PBX is a phone system in a box in your office. A cloud PBX does the same job from the provider’s data centre, over the internet. Both give a business extensions, transfers, menus, voicemail and ring groups. The difference is where the system runs and who looks after it. With a traditional PBX you buy the hardware, rent the lines and pay a technician to make changes. With a cloud PBX you pay a monthly fee for each line, staff answer on apps, computers or desk phones from anywhere, and you make changes yourself in a web portal.

Cloud PBX vs traditional PBX at a glance

Where it runs:
traditional: a unit in your building. Cloud: the provider’s data centre.
How you pay:
traditional: hardware up front, then lines and maintenance. Cloud: a monthly fee per line or number.
Working away from the office:
traditional: needs extra hardware and set-up. Cloud: built in, through apps and a browser.
If the office loses power or internet:
traditional: phones stop. Cloud: the system keeps answering and can send calls to mobiles.
Who it suits:
cloud: most small businesses. Traditional: a business with a recent PBX that still does what it needs, which can keep it and replace only the lines with a SIP trunk.

What is a PBX?

PBX stands for private branch exchange: a business’s own phone system. It gives each person an extension, lets calls be transferred, puts callers in a queue or a menu (“press 1 for sales”), and connects the business to the public phone network through a set of shared lines. Every multi-line business phone system is a PBX of some kind. What differs is where the PBX lives.

What is a traditional (on-premise) PBX?

A traditional PBX is a physical unit installed in your office, usually in a comms cupboard. Desk phones are cabled to it, and it connects to the phone network through lines rented from a carrier. Those lines used to be ISDN or analogue copper services. As the NBN replaced the copper network, those services were withdrawn, so an on-premise PBX today connects through a SIP trunk over the internet instead. Changes such as adding a person, renaming an extension or changing a menu are usually made by a technician.

What is a cloud PBX?

A cloud PBX, also called a hosted PBX or virtual PBX, is the same kind of phone system run by a provider in its data centre. Your phones connect to it over the internet using VoIP (voice over internet protocol). Staff answer on an app on their mobile, in a browser on their computer, or on a desk phone, from the office or anywhere else. The business manages users, numbers, menus and business hours in a web portal. The provider looks after the servers, updates and connection to the phone network.

Is a cloud PBX cheaper than a traditional PBX?

For most small businesses, yes, because the biggest traditional costs disappear: the PBX unit, installation, the maintenance contract and the eventual replacement. A cloud system is a monthly fee instead. At Click2Call a Business Line is $25 a month ex GST for each person, with 300 minutes included. A team of five with a main number is $135 a month ex GST. Desk phones are optional, because the apps and the Web Phone are free. The full breakdown, including one-off costs, is in how much a business phone system costs in Australia.

A traditional PBX can still cost less if you already own a recent one that does everything you need. Then the only ongoing cost is the lines, and a SIP trunk replaces those for a flat monthly fee. Click2Call’s is $50 a month ex GST with 500 minutes and 2 channels.

What happens if the office loses power or internet?

With a traditional PBX, the system is in the building, so a power cut or line fault stops the phones. With a cloud PBX, the system keeps running in the data centre even when the office is dark. On Click2Call you can set calls to go to a mobile automatically when the office phones go offline, and ring a mobile alongside the desk phones. Staff on the mobile app can keep calling on mobile data. Read keeping calls coming in during an outage.

Either way, desk phones need power. Keep a charged mobile phone handy for emergency calls to 000.

Is call quality as good on a cloud PBX?

On a stable business internet connection, yes. Each call needs only about 100 kbps each way, so even a basic NBN plan carries several calls at once. Stability matters more than speed: a congested Wi-Fi network or an overloaded upload causes choppy audio. Most problems come from the local network, such as an old router or a router feature called SIP ALG, and both are fixable. See one-way audio and SIP ALG.

Can I keep my numbers and phones if I switch?

Usually, yes. In Australia your current phone company must port your number to a new one when asked, as long as the number is still active. That covers local, mobile, 1300 and 1800 numbers. Your number keeps working with the old provider until it moves, so don’t cancel the old service first. At Click2Call porting is $100 inc GST per number; a local number usually moves in 5 to 10 business days, and a 1300 or 1800 number can take up to 30 days. See number porting.

Many desk phones from the last decade work with a cloud PBX if they support SIP. Phones made to work only with one brand of PBX usually don’t. Check which phones can I use.

How do I move from a traditional PBX to a cloud PBX?

  1. Check your current contract for an exit fee and the notice period. This often decides the timing.
  2. List what the old system does: numbers, extensions, menus, ring groups, business hours, voicemail boxes and any integrations.
  3. Set up the new system on a new number first and test it, including after-hours rules and the outage forward.
  4. Port your numbers. The old system keeps taking calls until each number moves.
  5. Retire the old PBX and cancel the old lines only after every port has completed.

Click2Call’s switching guide covers each step. Done-for-you set-up is $300 ex GST for 1 to 3 lines, and free on approval for 4 or more lines.

Which should I choose?

  • Choose a cloud PBX if your PBX is old or due for replacement, if anyone works from home, on the road or across sites, or if you’d rather not maintain hardware. This fits most small businesses.
  • Keep your traditional PBX and add a SIP trunk if it is recent, does what you need, and you have someone to maintain it.
  • Use Microsoft Teams as the phone system if your team already works in Teams all day. A Teams Line connects it to Australian numbers.

Key terms

PBX (private branch exchange)
A business’s own phone system: extensions, transfers, menus, queues and voicemail.
On-premise PBX
A PBX installed as hardware in the business’s own building.
Cloud PBX / hosted PBX / virtual PBX
A PBX run by a provider in its data centre and used over the internet.
VoIP (voice over internet protocol)
Carrying phone calls as data over an internet connection.
SIP trunk
Phone lines delivered over the internet to a business’s own PBX.
ISDN
The digital business phone lines that on-premise PBXs used before the NBN.
Softphone
A phone in an app or browser instead of a physical handset.
Auto attendant
A menu that greets callers and sends them to the right person or team.
