Can You Call 000 From a VoIP Phone?

Yes, you can call 000 from a VoIP business phone in Australia, and it’s free. What happens in a power or internet outage, how emergency services find you, and what a business should do.

Reviewed by Royce Clark, Director · Last checked 27 November 2026

By Royce Clark • Published 27 Nov 2026

Yes, you can call 000 from a VoIP business phone in Australia, and the call is free. But a VoIP phone needs power and a working internet connection, and the operator can’t always see where you are. If the office loses power or internet, a VoIP desk phone can’t call 000 at all. From a VoIP or mobile phone, the operator will ask for your town and state. The safest setup is simple: keep a charged mobile phone handy, keep your service address up to date, and never block emergency calls.

VoIP and 000 at a glance

Can you call 000?
Yes, from desk phones and apps on most VoIP services, including Click2Call lines. Calls to 000 are free from any phone.
In a power or internet outage:
a VoIP phone won’t work. Use a charged mobile.
Your location:
from a VoIP or mobile phone, the operator asks your town and state. If you can’t speak, police use the address held for the service.
Best phone for 000:
a mobile, using its own phone app. It works on any network with coverage, and some phones send their location automatically.
Other numbers:
112 works from mobiles only; 106 is for TTY users; the SES is 132 500.

Can I call 000 from a VoIP phone?

Yes. ACMA, the communications regulator, says you can call 000 from any fixed or mobile phone and from certain VoIP and satellite services. Calls to 000 are free from any kind of phone.

On Click2Call, calls to 000 are free, and they work from desk phones and phone apps on your lines unless someone has blocked them. Two exceptions:

  • The Web Phone is not for 000. It runs in a browser, can’t share your location and depends on your computer and internet. Use a mobile or landline.
  • Call barring can block 000. The portal lets you bar emergency calls on a line. Don’t: leave emergency calls open on any phone someone might grab in a hurry. See call barring.

What happens when you dial 000?

Telstra answers all calls to 000 and 112, then puts you through to the service you ask for. In major events such as bushfires or storms there may be a short wait: stay on the line, or you lose your place in the queue.

How do emergency services know where I am?

It depends on the phone:

  • Traditional fixed line: the address for the line appears automatically on the operator’s screen.
  • VoIP or mobile phone: the operator asks for your town and state, because you may not be at your home or billing address. Be ready to give the street address too.
  • If you can’t speak: police use the address for the service, usually the home or billing address, so it matters that your phone company has it right. On Click2Call, check the service address in the portal under Details.
  • Mobiles: select iPhone and Android phones send their location automatically (advanced mobile location). The free Emergency+ app, from the Apple App Store and Google Play, shows your GPS location (a street address or three words) to read out to the operator.

This is why a mobile is usually the best phone for 000 at a business, especially for staff who work from home or move between sites. A desk phone at one site can carry the address of another, but a mobile can give its own position.

What happens to 000 in a power or internet outage?

A VoIP phone stops working if the power or the internet goes down, because the phone, the router and the NBN connection all need power. ACMA notes that most services on the NBN won’t work during a power cut, so you won’t be able to call 000 from them. Keep another way to call, such as a charged mobile phone.

A cloud phone system can still take incoming calls during an office outage, by forwarding them to a mobile. That doesn’t help you call out: for 000, use the mobile itself. See keeping calls coming in during an outage.

What should a business do?

  1. Keep a charged mobile on site and tell staff to use it for 000 if the desk phones are down.
  2. Never bar emergency calls on any phone.
  3. Keep the service address up to date, and update it when you move.
  4. Put the street address near the phones, including the suburb and nearest cross street, so anyone can read it out.
  5. Tell people working from home to call 000 from their mobile, not a desk phone or app registered to the office.
  6. For clinics and care providers: start the phone menu with a short message telling callers to hang up and dial 000 in an emergency. See phone systems for medical clinics.

What other emergency numbers are there?

NumberWhat it’s for
000Police, fire or ambulance, from any phone. Free.
112Emergency number that works from mobile phones only.
106Text-based emergency calls from a TTY, for people who are deaf or have a hearing or speech impairment. The National Relay Service can also connect relay calls to 000.
132 500State Emergency Service (SES), for storm and flood help. 000 can’t transfer you to the SES.
911Doesn’t work in Australia.

You can’t text 000 or 112. Triple Zero has no translators on the first call: callers who don’t speak English are put through to police in their capital city, who arrange one.

Key terms

Triple Zero (000)
Australia’s main emergency number for police, fire and ambulance.
VoIP (voice over internet protocol)
Phone calls carried over an internet connection, as on a cloud phone system.
Service address
The address your phone company holds for a phone service, used by police if a 000 caller can’t speak.
Advanced mobile location (AML)
A feature of many smartphones that sends the phone’s coordinates during a 000 call.
Emergency+
A free app that shows your GPS location to read out to the 000 operator.
National Relay Service (NRS)
The service that helps people who are deaf or have a hearing or speech impairment make calls, including to emergency services.
