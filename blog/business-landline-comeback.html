Why the Business Landline Is Making a Comeback in Australia

Australian small businesses ditched the landline for mobiles. Now AI is bringing it back: call recording, transcripts, sentiment and an AI receptionist that a mobile number can't give you.

By Royce Clark October 8, 2026

For most of the last decade, the advice to small business owners was simple: drop the landline. The mobile went everywhere, the landline sat on a desk, and paying for both felt like paying twice. Sole traders, tradies, consultants and growing teams across Australia did exactly that, and printed a mobile number on the van, the website and the business card.

That trend is now reversing. Australian small and medium businesses are adding a business landline number again, and the reason isn't nostalgia. It's AI. A business number that runs through a cloud phone system can now record, transcribe, score and answer your calls for you, and a mobile number simply can't.

Why did businesses give up the landline?

Businesses dropped the landline because the old copper line was tied to one desk, cost a monthly line rental and did nothing a mobile couldn't. When the NBN replaced the copper network, keeping a landline meant extra cost for less flexibility, so many owners moved everything to one mobile number and never looked back.

For a while, that was the right call. A mobile number travels with you, it works on site, and one device is simpler than two.

What changed? AI made the phone call valuable

What changed is that every phone call can now become useful business data. A modern business landline is not a copper line; it is a local number hosted in the cloud. Because every call passes through the phone system, the system can record it, turn it into text, summarise it and act on it. Calls that ring straight to a personal mobile skip all of that.

When a customer rings your mobile, the call disappears the moment you hang up. There is no recording, no transcript and no record of what was promised, unless you write it down yourself. When the same customer rings a business number, every call is captured automatically.

What can a business landline do that a mobile number can't?

A cloud business landline records every call, emails you an AI transcript and summary, scores the caller's sentiment, takes a message with an AI answering agent when you can't pick up, and sends you an alert when a call is missed. A personal mobile number does none of this. Here's what each one means for a small business.

Every call recorded

Disputes over a quote, a booking or a promised call-back are settled in seconds when you can replay the conversation. On Click2Call, call recording is included for every user and runs automatically.

An AI transcript in your inbox

After each call, you receive an email with the recording, a full written transcript and a plain-English summary. You can search past calls by what was said, forward a transcript to a colleague, or copy the details straight into a quote. See AI Voice Tools.

Sentiment analysis on every caller

Each call is scored for how the caller felt. An unhappy customer stands out before they leave a bad review, and you can see whether your team's calls are trending better or worse over time.

An AI receptionist for overflow and after hours

When you're on the tools, in a meeting or closed for the night, calls don't have to ring out. Click2Call's AI Answer Agent rings your phone first. If you don't pick up, it takes the caller's name, number and reason, confirms them back, and emails you a transcript and summary. The AI Receptionist can also answer every call and send the caller to the right person. Both are included free with every line.

AI voicemail and voicemail to email

Professional voicemail greetings can be created with an AI voice in minutes, with no recording studio and no awkward takes. Every message arrives in your email with a transcript, so you can read it in a meeting instead of listening. See setting up AI voicemail.

Missed call alerts

If a call goes unanswered, you get an email or text straight away with who rang and when, so you can call back before they try the next business on their list. See missed call alerts.

One number for the whole business

A business number can ring several people at once, step through staff one after another, or queue callers with music. When you hire your first staff member or sell the business, the number stays with the business, not with your personal phone. See ring groups and hunt groups.

Do I have to give up my mobile?

No. You keep your mobile. The business landline rings the Secure VoIP app on your phone, so you answer customers anywhere, exactly as you do now. The difference is that the call goes through the business number first, so it is recorded, transcribed and answered by the AI agent when you can't pick up. Your personal number stays private.

The bottom line

The landline didn't come back because businesses missed the old phone on the desk. It came back because a business number is now the only way to capture, understand and automatically answer every customer call. For a small business, those calls are leads, bookings and promises. Letting them vanish into a personal mobile is no longer the cheap option. It's the expensive one.
