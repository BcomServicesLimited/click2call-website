Telstra DOT and T-Biz Voice End on 30 August 2027: What Your Business Needs to Do

Telstra is closing DOT and T-Biz Voice on the nbn. You can keep your phone numbers if you move them in time. Here is how, the dates to act by, and what the terms say about exit fees.

Reviewed by Royce Clark, Director · Last checked 28 November 2026

By Royce Clark • Published 28 Nov 2026

Telstra is closing two small-business phone products, DOT (Digital Office Technology) and T-Biz Voice on the nbn, and both stop working after 30 August 2027. You can keep your phone numbers, but only by moving them to a new provider while the Telstra service is still active. Lodge the number move by 1 June 2027, and don’t cancel anything with Telstra until every number has arrived. Telstra’s notice says nothing about what happens to phone numbers on the exit date, so treat 30 August 2027 as the day a number left behind is at risk. DOT also carries your internet, and sometimes your mobiles, so those need a new provider too.

What is ending, and when?

Telstra DOT and T-Biz Voice on the nbn both end on 30 August 2027. Telstra’s notice to customers lists the two products as “Telstra Business Internet (DOT)” and “T-Biz Voice on the nbn”, each with an exit date of 30 August 2027.

Telstra’s wording is: “You can continue using your current services until 30 August 2027. After that, the service will no longer operate.”

Telstra says DOT and T-Biz Voice on the nbn “are being retired due to hardware limitations”. Telstra’s notice does not name a replacement product.

Can I keep my phone numbers?

Yes. A business on Telstra DOT or T-Biz Voice can keep its phone numbers by moving them to a new provider before the service ends on 30 August 2027. Moving a number is called porting. The rules are published by ACMA, the Australian communications regulator, and apply whichever provider you move to:

  • Only an active number can be moved. ACMA says “you cannot port a disconnected number”, and that once a service is disconnected “you no longer have rights to the number”. This is why you must not cancel first.
  • The new provider runs the move. You give your permission, and the new provider organises it with Telstra. You don’t ask Telstra to release the number yourself.
  • Telstra must let the number go. ACMA’s rules say the old phone company “cannot refuse or delay porting the number even if your customer owes you money”.
  • Allow up to 30 days for several numbers. ACMA says single local numbers are generally moved “within 8 to 15 days”, and that “more complex ports (more than one local number) can take up to 30 days”.
  • Your old provider may charge a fee. ACMA notes the previous phone company might charge for porting, and you still pay its final bills.

Three practical steps:

  1. Get a full list of every number on the account. Ask Telstra for it in writing. Include direct lines, fax and EFTPOS numbers, and alarm lines. Some numbers are not printed prominently on a bill. For example, the DOT terms say that when you add a Hunt Group you are given a new Direct Business Line number, and your main number is forwarded to it.
  2. Use the exact account details from a recent bill. A move is rejected if the account name or number doesn’t match Telstra’s records. For a business, that usually means the registered name, not the trading name.
  3. Keep the Telstra service running until every number has moved. Your numbers stay live with Telstra during the move, so calls keep reaching you. Cancel what is left only after the last number has arrived.

Telstra’s notice doesn’t say what happens to phone numbers on 30 August 2027. Don’t wait to find out. Ask Telstra in writing, and plan to have every number moved well before that date.

When do I need to move my numbers by?

A business on Telstra DOT or T-Biz Voice should choose its new provider by 1 March 2027 and lodge its number move by 1 June 2027. Those two dates are Click2Call’s suggestion, worked back from Telstra’s exit date of 30 August 2027. Here is the reasoning:

  • 30 August 2027 is the last day the Telstra service operates.
  • 30 days is how long ACMA says a move of more than one local number can take. Counting back 30 days gives 31 July 2027. A move lodged that late has no room for anything to go wrong.
  • Another 30 days covers a rejection. A move is rejected if the account details don’t match Telstra’s records, and it then has to be corrected and lodged again.
  • A final 30 days is a safety margin, for slow replies and for numbers that turn up late on the account.

That is 90 days before the exit date, which is 1 June 2027. Lodging earlier is better, and nothing stops you moving your numbers months ahead. Treat 31 July 2027 as the last possible date to lodge, not the target.

The decide-by date of 1 March 2027 leaves three months before lodging. That time is for getting written answers from Telstra about exit fees, setting up and testing the new phone system and, for DOT customers, getting a new internet service running. Ask the new internet provider for its lead time.

What can and can’t I do now?

DOT and T-Biz Voice customers can keep using the service until 30 August 2027, but can’t change it. From Telstra’s notice:

  • You can keep using it until 30 August 2027.
  • You can’t change it. “No changes are allowed from 30 September 2025. You won’t be able to add, move, or recontract these services after this date.”
  • You can cancel any time before the exit date. Don’t do this until your numbers have moved (see above).
  • You can ask Telstra for help. Its notice says to contact your Telstra representative or call 1800 926 478.

What does DOT include, and what needs replacing?

Telstra DOT is three services on one bill (phone, internet and mobile), so each one needs a new provider before 30 August 2027. Telstra’s DOT terms describe it as a bundle of broadband “with IP telephony and mobile services on one account and one bill”.

  • Phone: your business numbers and the lines your staff answer.
  • Internet: the broadband connection for the office. When DOT ends, this ends with it.
  • Mobile: any mobile services that sit on the DOT account. Ask Telstra which of your mobiles are part of DOT and which are on separate plans.

T-Biz Voice on the nbn is a phone service. Telstra describes it as “a digital voice service” delivered over the nbn. If you have T-Biz Voice, ask Telstra whether anything else on your account is affected.

The three parts don’t have to go to the same company. Some providers sell all three. Others, including Click2Call, sell the phone part only, and it runs over whichever internet service you choose.

Will I pay exit fees?

Exit fees for leaving Telstra DOT or T-Biz Voice depend on which product you have, so ask Telstra in writing what applies to your account. Here is what each set of Telstra terms says:

  • T-Biz Voice: the T-Biz Voice terms say plans “have no minimum term and no early termination charge”.
  • DOT: the DOT terms set a 24-month term for core plans and still contain an early termination formula: the monthly fee, times the months left in the contract, times 50%, plus any outstanding hardware repayments. Handsets bought on a repayment plan can add to that.

DOT has not allowed recontracting since 30 September 2025, so no 24-month term can have started after that date. Telstra’s notice doesn’t say whether early termination charges apply to customers who leave because the product is closing. Ask Telstra to confirm, in writing, your contract end date and any charge for leaving.

On ordinary charges, Telstra’s notice says: “Billing stops once the service is cancelled. Charges are pro-rated if cancelled mid-cycle.”

What about fax, EFTPOS, alarms and lifts?

Check every line before you move anything. A phone line can carry more than phone calls. DOT offered add-ons for EFTPOS and fax, and an Alarm Line for a monitored security alarm. Older offices may also have a lift phone or a medical alarm on a phone line.

Telstra’s own terms say these devices may be supported but that it can’t guarantee they will work faultlessly, and tell customers to check with the equipment’s maker or provider.

Not every phone provider handles these devices, so ask any new provider before you move. If the new provider doesn’t handle them, each device needs a separate solution:

  • EFTPOS: ask your bank or terminal supplier how the terminal can connect without a phone line.
  • Alarms and lift phones: ask the monitoring company or lift contractor what the equipment needs.
  • Fax: ask the people who still fax you whether email will do. If not, you need a fax service.

Sort these out before you cancel the Telstra service, and tell your new phone provider which numbers to move and which to leave alone.

What are my options?

A business leaving Telstra DOT or T-Biz Voice has three options, and any of them can be right:

  1. Stay with Telstra on a newer product. Telstra’s notice says it is focusing on “scalable, cloud-based solutions” but names no product. Ask your Telstra representative what they offer, the contract term and the monthly cost. This keeps one supplier and one bill.
  2. Move everything to one other provider. Some Australian providers sell business phone systems, internet and mobiles together.
  3. Split the services. Choose a phone provider, an internet provider and a mobile provider separately.

Questions to ask any provider: the contract term, exit fees, what it charges to move a number, how long a move of several numbers takes, whether it handles fax, EFTPOS and alarm lines, and where call recordings are stored.

What is not known?

Some facts about the closure have not been published. As at 28 November 2026:

  • How many businesses are affected. We found no published figure for the number of DOT or T-Biz Voice customers.
  • What happens to phone numbers on 30 August 2027. Telstra’s notice does not mention phone numbers.
  • Which product replaces DOT or T-Biz Voice. Telstra’s notice names none.
  • Whether DOT early termination charges apply to customers leaving because of the closure. Telstra’s notice does not say. The DOT terms still contain the charge.

Telstra can answer the last three for your own account. Ask in writing.

Checklist: moving off DOT or T-Biz Voice

  • Find out which product you have: DOT, T-Biz Voice, or both.
  • Ask Telstra in writing for a full list of numbers on the account.
  • Ask Telstra in writing for your contract end date and any charge for leaving.
  • List every device on a phone line: fax, EFTPOS, alarm, lift phone, medical alarm.
  • Arrange a separate solution for each of those devices with its supplier.
  • DOT only: choose a new internet provider and get the service running.
  • DOT only: decide where any mobiles on the account are going.
  • Choose a new phone provider by 1 March 2027. Set it up and test it on a temporary number.
  • Lodge the number move by 1 June 2027, using the exact account details from a Telstra bill. Attach your Telstra bill.
  • Do not cancel the Telstra service while any number is still moving.
  • When every number has arrived, test incoming and outgoing calls on each one.
  • Then cancel what is left with Telstra, before 30 August 2027.

Key terms

DOT (Digital Office Technology)
A Telstra small-business bundle of broadband, internet phone lines and mobile services on one account and one bill. Telstra’s notice lists it as Telstra Business Internet (DOT).
T-Biz Voice on the nbn
A Telstra business phone service delivered over the nbn. Telstra’s terms also call it Business Voice on nbn.
End of sale
The date a product stops being sold or changed. For both products, 30 September 2025.
Exit date (end of life)
The date a product stops working. For both products, 30 August 2027.
Porting
Moving a phone number from one phone company to another, so you keep the number.
Early termination charge
A fee for leaving a contract before its term ends.
ACMA
The Australian Communications and Media Authority, the regulator that sets the number porting rules.
