Missed Calls in Australian Small Business: What 7,895 Calls Show

One in six calls goes unanswered, and only one in five of those callers gets a call back. Findings from 7,895 calls to 123 Australian businesses.

By Royce Clark November 21, 2026

When a customer rings a small business and nobody picks up, what happens next? We looked at 7,895 incoming calls to 123 Australian businesses to find out. One in six calls went unanswered. Of those callers, only one in five got a call back within a day.

The short version

  • 16% of calls to a small business go unanswered.
  • 33% of calls arrive outside Monday to Friday, 9 to 5.
  • 62% of callers who don’t get through try again within the hour.
  • 20% get a call back from the business within 24 hours.
  • 47% still haven’t spoken with the business a week later.
  • 61% of first-time callers whose first call wasn’t answered never got through at all.

How many calls to a small business go unanswered?

About one in six. Of 7,895 incoming calls, 1,281 (16%) were never answered: the call reached the business’s number, and the caller gave up before a person, voicemail or answering service picked up. This is a measure of answering, not of the phone line: a call counts as unanswered when it ended before anyone or anything picked up. During business hours the figure was slightly higher, at 17%.

It varies a lot between businesses. The typical business in the sample lost 12% of its calls this way, but more than a third of businesses lost more than one call in five.

When do customers call?

A third of calls (33%) arrive outside Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm. Most of those come on weekday evenings: 17% of all calls arrive at 5pm or later on a weekday. Another 10% come on weekends, and 6% before 9am.

Thursday was the busiest day for incoming calls, followed by Friday. Monday, often assumed to be the peak, came fourth.

What time of day are calls most likely to be missed?

Late morning and mid-afternoon. On weekdays, 24% of calls at 11am and 24% at 3pm went unanswered. At 9am the figure was 9%. The calls most likely to be missed are not the after-hours ones. They arrive in the middle of the working day, when the people who would answer are with a customer, on a job or on another call.

What do callers do when they can’t get through?

Most try again, and quickly. After an unanswered call, 62% of callers rang back within the hour. About half of those second attempts worked: 32% of callers had got through within an hour of the first try, and 44% within 24 hours.

Businesses return far fewer of these calls. Only 20% of callers who didn’t get through received a call back from the business within 24 hours.

A week after the first unanswered call, 47% of callers still had not got through to the business, or been reached by it. For 23%, neither side tried again.

What happens to first-time callers?

First-time callers fare worst, and they are the ones most likely to be new customers. In the sample, 264 people called a business for the first time and weren’t answered. Of those, 160 (61%) never got through afterwards, and the business never reached them either.

An existing customer will usually try again, send a text or email, or wait. Someone ringing for a quote has no reason to wait, and other businesses to call.

What can a small business do about missed calls?

The figures point to four practical steps. None of them needs more staff.

  1. Know your own number. Most owners guess how many calls they miss. Turn on missed call alerts so every unanswered call reaches you by email or text, with the caller’s number.
  2. Return missed calls the same day. Four in five callers who don’t get through never hear back within a day. Calling back within the hour, while the caller is still trying, is the single biggest gain.
  3. Cover the busy hours, not just after hours. The worst hours are 11am and 3pm. Let calls roll to a second person, a ring group or your mobile when the first phone isn’t answered.
  4. Make sure something always answers. A third of calls come outside 9 to 5. Voicemail is the minimum. An AI receptionist can take the caller’s name, number and reason, and email it to you.

How we worked this out

We analysed the records of 7,895 incoming calls to 123 Click2Call business accounts between 11 March and 20 November 2026. Two-thirds of the calls were made from September to November. The records hold the time, length and numbers of each call. They do not hold what was said, and we did not use recordings or transcripts.

No business, caller or phone number is identified in this report, in line with our privacy policy.

The limits of the figures:

  • These are Click2Call customers, mostly businesses of one to ten people. They are not a random sample of all Australian small businesses.
  • “Unanswered” means the call lasted zero seconds. That includes callers who hung up early and some nuisance calls. The records don’t show why a call ended.
  • “Answered” includes voicemail and automated answering. The share of callers who reached a person is lower than the answered figures here, so the week-later and first-time-caller findings understate the problem.
  • A few busy accounts make up a large share of calls. The three busiest hold 27%. Without them, 19% of calls went unanswered and 28% arrived outside business hours.
  • Local time comes from the area code of the number called, which was known for 98% of calls. The rest were treated as Brisbane time.
  • Call-back figures cover 1,140 unanswered calls where the caller’s number was shown and at least a week of later records was available.
