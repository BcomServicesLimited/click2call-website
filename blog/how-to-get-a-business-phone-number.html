How to Get a Business Phone Number in Australia: Number Types Explained

How to get a business phone number in Australia: local 02, 03, 07, 08, 04 mobile, 1300 and 1800 numbers compared, with costs, porting and smartnumbers explained.

Reviewed by Royce Clark, Director · Last checked 26 November 2026

By Royce Clark • Updated 26 Nov 2026

To get a business phone number in Australia, choose the type of number you want, sign up with a phone provider, and either take a new number or bring across one you already have. There are four types small businesses use: a local number starting 02, 03, 07 or 08; an 04 mobile number; a 1300 number, where callers pay a local rate; and an 1800 number, which is free for callers. With an online provider the number can be working the same day, and it rings an app on your mobile or computer, so nothing needs to be installed.

Business phone numbers at a glance

Who sets the rules:
the Australian Communications and Media Authority (ACMA) manages Australia’s numbers and gives them to phone companies, which give them to customers.
The four common types:
local (02, 03, 07, 08), mobile (04), local rate (1300) and freephone (1800).
What a new one costs:
at Click2Call, $10 a month ex GST for any type. A local number is free to allocate, an 04 number is $50 inc GST once, and a 1300 or 1800 number is $100 inc GST once.
Keeping your number:
local, mobile, 1300 and 1800 numbers can usually be ported to a new provider.
How long it takes:
a new number is usually live within the hour. A ported mobile number usually moves in about 3 hours, and a local number in days rather than hours.

What types of business phone numbers are there in Australia?

ACMA lists six main types of phone number in Australia. Four of them are the ones businesses choose between. The other two are 13 numbers, a short form of local rate number, and 09 numbers, which are for internet-of-things devices rather than people.

What do the area codes 02, 03, 07 and 08 mean?

A local number is ten digits: a two-digit area code, then eight digits. The area code tells callers roughly where you are.

On a cloud phone system the number is not tied to a building, so a Brisbane 07 number can be answered from anywhere in Australia. You can hold a number for each city you serve. You can also have a regional number for your own town, such as 02 49 for Newcastle or 07 46 for Toowoomba. The regional numbers list shows common prefixes.

Local, 1300 or 1800: which should I choose?

  • Choose a local number if most customers are in one city or region. It is free to allocate, incoming calls cost you nothing, and a local code matches what people expect from a Google Maps listing.
  • Choose a 1300 number if you serve the whole country and want one number with no city attached. Callers pay a local rate, which most mobile plans include, and you pay for the incoming call.
  • Choose an 1800 number if calling must cost the caller nothing, for example on a support line or in an advertising campaign. You pay for the incoming call.
  • Choose an 04 mobile number if you want to text customers from the same business number you take calls on.

Many businesses use two: a local number on the Google Business Profile and a 1300 number on the website, vans and print. 1300 and 1800 numbers receive calls only. Under ACMA’s rules, 13, 1300 and 1800 numbers can’t be shown as caller ID, so staff call out from a local or mobile number. The 1300 and 1800 numbers page compares them side by side.

Can I get an 04 business mobile number without a second phone?

Yes. A business 04 number can live in an app rather than on a SIM, so one handset carries both your personal and business numbers. On Click2Call an 04 number is the only type that sends and receives SMS, at 20c a message ex GST, which suits booking confirmations and job updates. A new 04 number is $50 inc GST once, then $10 a month ex GST.

What is a smartnumber or a 13 number?

A smartnumber is a memorable 13, 1300 or 1800 number: a pattern such as 1300 222 222, or a phoneword that spells a word on the keypad. ACMA sells smartnumbers directly through its online numbering system. The one-off charge depends on how memorable the number is. Under the Allocation Charge Determination 2025 it ranges from $250 for a standard 1300 or 1800 number, or $400 for a 13 number, up to $20,000 for the most sought-after 1300 and 1800 patterns. Smartnumbers also attract ACMA’s Annual Numbering Charge, and you still need a phone provider to connect a service to the number.

A 13 number is the six-digit version of a local rate number, used by national brands that advertise heavily. For most small businesses an ordinary 1300 or 1800 number from a provider does the same job for much less.

Can I keep my existing number?

Usually, yes. In Australia you can port a local, mobile, 1300 or 1800 number to a new provider, as long as the number is still active. A disconnected number can’t be ported. ACMA says a mobile port usually takes about 3 hours. A single local number generally takes 8 to 15 days, and more complex ports can take up to 30 days. Your number keeps working with the old provider until it moves, so don’t cancel the old service first.

Should I just use my personal mobile number?

It is free, but it causes problems later. A personal number on your website gets scraped by marketing lists, rings at 8pm on a Sunday, and can’t be handed to the first person you hire. A business number belongs to the business. It can ring different people at different times, have its own voicemail and after-hours greeting, and move with the business if you change providers.

What should I set up once the number works?

  • Business hours and an after-hours greeting. You can type a greeting and have AI Speech read it in a natural voice instead of recording it yourself.
  • Voicemail to email, so messages reach you wherever you are.
  • An AI receptionist. It answers in your business name and puts callers through by what they say. When nobody can answer, it takes the caller’s name, number and reason, and emails you a transcript.
  • Your Google Business Profile, updated with the new number so it matches your website.

Key terms

Geographic number
A local number starting 02, 03, 07 or 08, linked to a region of Australia.
Local rate number
A 13 or 1300 number. Callers pay a local call rate and the business pays for the rest of the call.
Freephone number
An 1800 or 180 number. The business pays the whole call, so it is free for the caller.
Inbound service
What phone companies call a 13, 1300 or 1800 number. It receives calls only.
Smartnumber
A memorable 13, 1300 or 1800 number sold by ACMA, such as a pattern or phoneword.
Porting
Moving an existing number from one phone company to another without changing it.
Virtual number
A number that lives on a cloud phone system rather than a physical line, and rings apps or other phones.
CLI (calling line identification)
The number the person you call sees on their screen.
