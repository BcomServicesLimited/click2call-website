Click2Call Web Phone: Calls from Your Browser

The Click2Call Web Phone lets you make and take calls on your business number from a web browser, with nothing to download. Open phone.click2call.com.au, sign in with your line's SIP username and password, and you're ready to call. This guide covers every feature, the best setups, and what the Web Phone can't do. For an overview, see the Web Phone page.

Last reviewed: November 2026

Open the Web Phone

What you need

  • Your line's SIP username and password. Find them in the portal under Voice → Line Manager. The username is your number starting with 61 and no plus (for example 61712345678), or an extension line ID (for example 832800190000). See account and line passwords if you need to reset it.
  • A current browser: Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox on Mac or Windows. Chrome or Edge is best if you want to install it as an app.
  • A microphone and speaker. A USB or Bluetooth headset gives the clearest calls.
  • A steady internet connection. NBN, office broadband or a good 4G/5G signal all work.
Cost: there's no extra charge for the Web Phone. Calls are billed exactly like calls from any other device on your line, and incoming calls are free.

Signing in

1

Open the Web Phone

Go to phone.click2call.com.au in your browser.

2

Enter your SIP username and password, or scan the QR code

Type them exactly as Line Manager shows them. Or tap Scan QR code and point your camera at the QR code on the line's page in Line Manager (the same one the Basic Edition app uses). The Web Phone reads it and signs you in, with nothing to type. This is quickest on a mobile while Line Manager is open on your computer. The code is read on your device and the camera image isn't sent anywhere. Scan it from inside the Web Phone: your phone's own camera app opens a different app. The QR code contains your line's password, so don't share a screenshot of it. Leave Keep me signed in on this computer ticked on your own computer, so next time it opens straight to the keypad. Untick it on a shared computer.

3

Allow the microphone and notifications

Your browser asks for microphone access the first time. Choose Allow. If it also asks about notifications, allow those too so you see incoming calls when the window is behind others.

4

Check for the green dot

The status line under the logo shows a green dot and Ready with your number. You can now make and receive calls.

Features

Making a call

Type the number on the keypad or your keyboard and press the green call button (or Enter). Dial Australian numbers as you would on any phone, for example 0412 345 678, 07 5555 1234 or 1300 numbers. A number starting with +61 is converted for you. Your Click2Call number shows as the caller ID.

Answering a call

When a call comes in, the Web Phone rings and shows the caller's number with Answer and Decline buttons. If the window is hidden, you also get a desktop notification. Click it to bring the phone forward. Declining sends the caller straight to your line's busy settings (usually voicemail), and stops the call ringing on your other phones too.

During a call

  • Mute: the other person can't hear you until you tap it again.
  • Keypad: sends touch tones, for menus like "press 1 for accounts". You can also type the digits on your keyboard.
  • Hold: puts the caller on hold. Tap again to return.
  • Transfer: type a number in Transfer to…, then choose:
    • Transfer hands the call straight over (blind transfer).
    • Talk first puts your caller on hold and rings the other person so you can introduce the call. Press Complete transfer to connect them, or Cancel to go back to your caller.
  • Call waiting: if another call comes in while you're talking, you hear a soft beep and see it under your call. Hold & answer puts your first caller on hold, and Swap moves between the two calls. A third call gets a busy signal.
  • Call timer and connection warning: the timer shows how long you've been talking. A Poor connection warning appears if your internet is dropping audio.

Recents

The Recents tab lists your calls with the time and duration, like the call list on a mobile. Missed calls show in red, and a red badge counts any you haven't seen. Tap the phone icon to call back, or tap the number to put it on the keypad first. Clear empties the list. Recents only shows calls made or received in this browser on this computer. For your full account history, see call records in the portal.

Click-to-call from other apps

In Chrome or Edge, open Settings and press Use Web Phone for phone number links, then choose Allow. When you click a phone number in your CRM, email or on a website, a small Call …? screen opens. Press Call and the call opens in a new tab, while the page you were on stays open in its own tab, so you can keep reading it during the call. When you hang up, the call tab closes by itself and the call is added to Recents. It never dials until you press Call. If you're not signed in on that computer, the full Web Phone opens instead with the number ready.

Settings

Click the gear icon to:

  • Choose your microphone and speaker, for example a headset rather than the laptop speakers. Choosing a speaker works in Chrome and Edge. Safari and Firefox always use your computer's default output.
  • Turn on Do not disturb. The Web Phone stops ringing and logs incoming calls as missed. Your other phones on the line, such as a desk phone or the Basic Edition app, keep ringing as normal. If nothing answers, the call goes to your voicemail or forward rules after about a minute. An orange tag shows while it's on.
  • Install the app and see the steps to open it at login (below).
  • Sign out. This disconnects the line and forgets your saved sign-in on this computer.

Keep me signed in

With this ticked, the Web Phone opens straight to the keypad each time. Your password is encrypted and saved only in this browser on this computer, and never on our servers. The encryption key can't be copied off the computer, so copying the browser's files elsewhere won't reveal your password. Like an unlocked desk phone, anyone using that computer can make calls, so untick it on shared computers. If your SIP password changes, the Web Phone asks you to sign in again.

Ring me when the app is closed

Turn on Settings → Set up this computer → Rings when the app is closed and calls reach you even when the Web Phone isn't open. You get an Incoming call notification with Answer and Decline. Press Answer and the Web Phone opens and connects the call by itself.

  • It keeps you signed in on that device, so a notification can open the phone straight into the call. On an installed app, the Web Phone also offers to turn this on once after you sign in.
  • Your browser must be running. On Windows and Mac, that means Chrome or Edge is open, even if the Web Phone isn't. On Android it works even when Chrome is closed. On iPhone it only works from the home-screen app, as a notification.
  • The alert repeats every 5 seconds, like a phone ringing, until you answer, press Decline, or the caller hangs up. When you answer on one device, the alert clears on your others. Decline stops the alerts on every device and sends the caller straight to your voicemail or forward rules.
  • If no one answers within about 60 seconds, the call goes to your voicemail or forward rules, even if your line's ring time in Line Manager is longer. If the caller hangs up first, you get a Missed call notification.
  • If you change your line's password, each device gets a Sign in to the Web Phone again notification, and calls go to your normal voicemail or forward rules until you do. Sign in with the new password (with Keep me signed in ticked) and it turns back on by itself.
  • Use Send a test notification in Settings to check it works on each device.
  • How it works: while it's on, our gateway keeps your line signed in for you. It stores only an encrypted hash of your SIP password, never the password itself (see Privacy and security below).
  • Forwarding when it's on: your line always shows as online, so Forward when Offline won't trigger. Use Forward on No Answer instead.
On a Mac, check notifications are allowed: open System Settings → Notifications → Google Chrome (and Google Chrome Helper (Alerts) if listed), turn on Allow notifications, and set the style to Alerts so the call stays on screen until you answer. A Focus or Do Not Disturb mode also hides them. Without this, Chrome receives the call but macOS doesn't show it. On Windows, check Settings → System → Notifications is on for Chrome or Edge.
On an iPhone, make sure the alert makes a sound:
  • Turn on Ring me when the app is closed from the home-screen app, not from Safari.
  • Open Settings → Notifications → C2C Phone. Turn on Allow Notifications, Lock Screen and Sounds, and set the banner style to Persistent. Make sure it isn't set to Deliver Quietly or Scheduled Summary.
  • Check the Ring/Silent switch (or Action button) isn't on silent. On silent, the alert shows but makes no sound. Set Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Haptics to Always Play so it still vibrates.
  • A Focus mode (Do Not Disturb, Sleep, Work) hides alerts unless C2C Phone is an allowed app in that Focus.
  • A phone lying face down may stay quiet, because iPhone quietens alerts when the screen is covered.

It's a notification rather than a full ringing screen. For calls on your mobile that ring like a normal phone, use the Basic Edition app.

Install it on your computer and open it at login

The best desktop setup is to install the Web Phone as an app, have it open automatically when you log in, and turn on Rings when the app is closed in Settings. It then runs in its own window with its own Dock or taskbar icon, like any other app.

Google Chrome (Mac or Windows)

  1. Sign in to the Web Phone, then click Install in the green banner. You can also use the install icon at the right of the address bar, or Settings → Install Click2Call Phone.
  2. To open it at login, open a new tab and go to chrome://apps, right-click Click2Call Phone and choose Start app when you sign in.
  3. Turn on Settings → Set up this computer → Rings when the app is closed in the Web Phone. If Chrome puts the phone to sleep in the background, calls still reach you as a notification.

Microsoft Edge (Windows or Mac)

  1. Click Install in the banner, or the app icon in the address bar.
  2. Go to edge://apps, click … on Click2Call Phone and turn on Auto-start on device login.
  3. Turn on Settings → Set up this computer → Rings when the app is closed in the Web Phone, so calls still reach you if Edge puts the phone to sleep.

Safari (Mac)

  1. With the Web Phone open, choose File → Add to Dock.
  2. Right-click the Click2Call Phone icon in the Dock and choose Options → Open at Login.

Firefox can't install web apps. If you use Firefox, keep the Web Phone in a pinned tab, or install it from Chrome, Edge or Safari instead.

Add it to your iPhone home screen

  1. Open Safari and go to phone.click2call.com.au. It must be Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button (the square with an arrow pointing up).
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen. If you see Open as Web App, leave it on, then tap Add.
  4. Open C2C Phone from your home screen and sign in once with Keep me signed in ticked (tap Scan QR code to sign in from Line Manager's QR code). Allow the microphone.

On Android, open the page in Chrome, tap the ⋮ menu and choose Add to Home screen or Install app.

Important for mobiles: phones pause web apps as soon as you switch away or lock the screen, so the Web Phone on a mobile only rings while it's open on screen. Call audio also plays through the loudspeaker on phones (browsers can't switch to the earpiece), so use earphones for private calls. It's handy for quick calls on the go. To receive calls on your mobile, use the Basic Edition app, which rings even when your phone is locked.

Forward calls to your mobile when the Web Phone isn't open

When the Web Phone is closed, your computer is asleep, or you've lost internet, it can't ring. Set a forward so those calls go to your mobile instead of being missed:

  1. Log in to the portal and go to Voice → Line Manager. Click your number to open its Call Flow page.
  2. Open the Incoming Calls dropdown and choose Call Forwarding.
  3. Under Forward when Offline, tick Enabled and enter your mobile. Use the 04 format, for example 0412345678, not 61 or +61. Save.
  4. Optional: also set Forward on No Answer to your mobile, so calls you don't answer in the Web Phone go on to your mobile.

"Offline" means nothing is signed in to the line. If the Basic Edition app or a desk phone is also signed in, the line isn't offline. In that case, use Forward on No Answer instead.

The Web Phone signs out about two minutes after you close it, so the forward starts working then. If your computer goes to sleep or loses internet without closing it, it can take up to about seven minutes. With Ring me when the app is closed on, your line stays online, so use Forward on No Answer instead.

What forwarding costs: a forwarded call leaves our platform, so the leg out to your mobile is billed as an outgoing call to a mobile from your number. It uses your included minutes first, then the standard mobile rate. See Forwarding Calls to Your Mobile for more forwarding options, including work-hours schedules.

Limitations

Please read these before relying on the Web Phone as your only phone.

  • Calls ring it while it's open, or with Ring me when the app is closed on, as long as the browser is running. If the computer is asleep, shut down or offline, calls can't reach it. Use Forward on No Answer or the Basic Edition app as a backup.
  • On iPhone and Android it only rings while open on screen, unless Ring me when the app is closed is on. Phones pause web apps in the background and when locked. With the setting on, you get a repeating notification instead of a full ringing screen. For calls that ring like a normal phone, use the Basic Edition app on your mobile. iPhone may also ask for microphone permission again after the app has been closed for a while.
  • On iPhone and Android, call audio plays through the loudspeaker. Mobile browsers don't let web pages switch between the earpiece and the speaker. For private calls on a mobile, use wired or Bluetooth earphones (the phone switches to them automatically), or the Basic Edition app, which has a normal speaker button.
  • Not for emergency calls. Don't use the Web Phone for 000. It can't share your location and depends on your computer and internet. Always call 000 from a mobile or landline.
  • Two calls at most, no conferencing. You can have one call active and one on hold or waiting. A third call goes to your voicemail or forward rules and shows as missed in Recents. There are no three-way conference calls.
  • Click-to-call needs Chrome or Edge on a computer. Safari and phones can't make the Web Phone the app for phone number links.
  • Recents is per device. It only shows calls made or received in that browser on that computer, and clearing your browser data removes it. Your full call history is in the portal.
  • Numbers only, no contact names. Incoming calls show the caller's number, not a name from your address book.
  • No voicemail, chat or video in the app. Voicemail still works as normal on your line and is emailed to you if you've set up voicemail-to-email.
  • Speaker choice needs Chrome or Edge. Safari and Firefox always play through your computer's default speaker.
  • Firefox can't install it as an app. Use a pinned tab, or Chrome, Edge or Safari.
  • Call quality depends on your internet. Weak Wi-Fi or a congested connection can cause choppy audio. You'll see a Poor connection warning. A wired connection or headset helps, and our call quality guide has more tips.
  • Strict workplace networks. The Web Phone uses secure web connections and switches to a relay automatically when a firewall blocks direct audio, so it works on most business networks. Very locked-down networks may need IT to allow phone.click2call.com.au and gw1.webphone.click2call.com.au.
  • Saved sign-in is per browser. Each browser, and the iPhone home-screen app, keeps its own sign-in, so you sign in once in each.

Built-in diagnostics

Open Settings → Diagnostics & self-test to see exactly what the Web Phone is doing:

  • Status: whether it's connected and signed in, plus microphone and notification permissions, Do not disturb, and your browser.
  • Run self-test: checks your microphone (with a sound level), the connection to our gateway, your sign-in, and both network paths: direct, and the relay used on strict firewalls.
  • Last call: round-trip time, jitter, packet loss, codec, and whether the audio went direct or through the relay.
  • Recent events: the last 100 sign-in and call events, such as REGISTER → 200 OK or INVITE → 486 Busy. They're kept in your browser for this session only, and passwords are never recorded. Report a problem (in Settings) sends a short description, your email for our reply, and this report (plus the last 20 full SIP messages, for our engineers) straight to Click2Call support. No email app needed.
  • Copy report: copies all of the above, ready to paste into an email to support@click2call.com.au.

The Web Phone also looks after itself. It reconnects as soon as your internet comes back or your computer wakes. It keeps a call going when you switch networks, for example from Wi-Fi to your phone's hotspot. It switches microphones if you unplug a headset mid-call. And it retries a call once by itself if it fails on a network blip before ringing.

Privacy and security

  • Calls are encrypted between your browser and Click2Call's gateway in Sydney, and connect to our Australian platform from there.
  • Your SIP password itself is never stored on our servers. To sign your line in for you, our gateway keeps an encrypted hash of it (a one-way fingerprint, not the password). It's deleted when you sign out of every device, or after 30 days without signing in. If you choose Keep me signed in, the password is encrypted and kept only in your browser on that computer.
  • Recents and your settings stay in your browser on that computer.
  • The Web Phone loads no advertising or tracking scripts.
