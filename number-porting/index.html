What Number Porting Actually Is

Porting is the regulated process of moving a phone number from one Australian carrier to another while keeping the number itself. It exists precisely so that your phone number belongs to your business rather than to whoever currently bills you for it — you're not locked to a provider just because they issued the number.

In practice it means you can move an established business number to a modern Cloud PBX without reprinting a single business card. Everything customers already have — the number on your vehicle, in your email signature, on Google, on ten years of invoices — keeps working exactly as it does today.

Porting From Telstra, Optus and Others

Number portability is regulated in Australia, which means every carrier is required to release your number when you lodge a valid port request. Telstra, Optus, TPG, Vocus, Aussie Broadband, Vonex and the rest all support it. Your current provider doesn't get a veto, and they can't hold the number hostage because they'd rather keep the account.

What they can do is reject a request with details that don't match their records — which is the single most common cause of porting delays. Copy your account number, account-holder name and service address straight off a recent bill rather than from memory, and most ports run through cleanly first time.

One important caveat that has nothing to do with porting: if you're inside a fixed contract term with your current provider, exiting may trigger an early-termination charge from them. That's between you and them — but it's worth checking before you lodge, so there are no surprises. Our Click2Call vs Telstra IP Telephony comparison covers the contract-term side in detail.

Number Porting Questions

How fast is number porting to Click2Call?
Local geographic (02/03/07/08) and Australian mobile numbers typically complete in 5–10 business days from lodgement. 1300 and 1800 numbers can take up to 30 days due to extra carrier coordination. There's no downtime at any stage — your number stays fully active with your current provider until it cuts over.
Can I port my number from Telstra or Optus?
Yes. Portability is regulated in Australia, so Telstra, Optus, TPG, Vocus, Aussie Broadband, Vonex and every other carrier must release your number when you lodge a valid request. They can't refuse simply because they'd prefer to keep your business.
Will my phone stop working during the port?
No — this is the most common worry and the answer is genuinely no. Your number runs on your current provider's network for the entire window while you build and test your Click2Call system on a temporary number. On cutover day calls simply start arriving at Click2Call instead. No outage.
Should I cancel my old service first?
No — never cancel first. If the service is disconnected before the port completes, the number can be permanently lost. Lodge the port and let it complete; the old service closes as part of the transfer. Check contract exit fees with your provider separately.
What details do I need to lodge a port?
Your current provider's account number, the account-holder name and service address exactly as they appear on your bill, and the list of numbers to move. Mismatched details are the number-one cause of rejected ports — copy them from a recent invoice, not from memory.
Can I port a mobile number to a business phone system?
Often yes — $50 ex GST. It's popular with sole traders: the mobile number customers already know becomes a full business phone system with call recording, an AI receptionist and after-hours routing. Mobile eligibility depends on your current carrier, so confirm with us before lodging.
Can I port some numbers but not others?
Yes — you can move your main advertised line and leave other services in place. Numbers that form part of a hunt group with your current carrier sometimes have to move together, so mention your setup when lodging and we'll confirm what's possible.
