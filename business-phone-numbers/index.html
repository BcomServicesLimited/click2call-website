Local numbers: 02, 03, 07 and 08

A local number tells callers you are nearby, which matters for trades, clinics and any business people find on a map. Choose 02 for Sydney and Canberra, 03 for Melbourne and Hobart, 07 for Brisbane and the Gold Coast, or 08 for Perth, Adelaide and Darwin. Local numbers are free to allocate, incoming calls are unlimited, and you can hold one number per city you serve. Many customers start here: it is the number that goes on the website and the Google Business Profile. Outside the capitals, you can also have a regional number for your own town at the same price.

04 mobile numbers

An 04 business mobile number lives in the app rather than on a SIM, so you carry one handset with two numbers on it. It is the only type that sends and receives SMS, at 20c a message, which suits booking confirmations and job updates. A new 04 number costs $50 inc GST once. If you would rather keep your current mobile number, porting it in is possible but depends on your carrier, and the number then stops working on the SIM and rings through the app instead.

1300 numbers

A 1300 number gives a business one number for the whole country without a city attached. Callers pay a local call rate, and you pay 20c a minute for the incoming call. 1300 numbers receive calls only, so most businesses pair one with a local number or a $25 user for calling out. A new 1300 number costs $100 inc GST once, then $10 a month. Care providers, trades covering several regions and any business that advertises nationally are the usual customers. The 1300 and 1800 numbers page goes into routing, porting and a worked example.

1800 numbers

An 1800 number is free for the caller from any Australian phone, which is why support lines and lead-generation campaigns use them. You pay 20c a minute for each incoming call, the same as a 1300 number, and the number receives calls only. Allocation is $100 inc GST once. If callers hesitate to ring because of cost, an 1800 number removes that hesitation; if not, a 1300 number does the same job at a local-call rate for them. When the $25 user is cheaper

A Cloud PBX user is $25 a month ex GST and includes a number plus 300 outbound minutes. The break-even is about 75 minutes a month of calls to mobiles, or about 150 minutes to landlines. Below that, the $10 number and pay-as-you-call is cheaper; above it, the $25 user is. The pricing calculator does the sum for your own call pattern.

The two plans share the same phone system. The only difference is how calls out are charged, so you can start on $10 and move a number to a $25 user later from the portal, or mix the two across a team: a $25 user for the person who calls all day, $10 numbers for the people who mostly answer.

An account takes two calls at once. Extra channels are $3 a month each, which a busy reception desk may want.

A business number on your mobile: the virtual landline explained

People search for this under several names: virtual landline, virtual business phone number, virtual phone number, business number for a mobile. They all describe the same thing. A real Australian number, with a real area code, that is not tied to a socket in a wall. Calls to it travel over the internet to wherever you have told the number to ring.

For a sole trader the practical effect is simple. You keep the mobile you already own. The Secure VoIP app rings for business calls and shows the business number when you dial out. Your personal number never appears on an invoice or a van, and when you sell the business or hire someone, the number goes with the business rather than with your phone.

Compared with giving out a personal mobile, a business number records calls if you switch recording on, transcribes them, routes them to a colleague when you are on another call, and answers with the AI when you cannot. Compared with an old landline, there is no line rental, no installation and nothing to move when you change premises. A business number without a landline is the normal case now, not the exception.

If you prefer not to use the app, the number can simply forward to your mobile's own number. A forwarded call is billed as an outbound call, so it uses a $25 user's included minutes first and otherwise costs 20c a minute to mobiles. Most people use the app once they have tried it, because caller ID and recording only work that way.

On your website and your Google Business Profile

The business number is what goes on the website, the Google Business Profile, directories, invoices and signage. A local number matches what people expect to see on a map listing for a local business; a 1300 or 1800 number suits a business that does not want to look tied to one suburb. Because the number is yours and portable, you can put it everywhere without worrying that a change of provider will force you to reprint anything.

One number can also sit behind a tracking plan: a second $10 number used only in advertising tells you which calls came from the ads, with the call reports in the portal showing each number separately.

Keeping the number you already have

If your number is already on the ute and the website, bring it with you. Local, 04 mobile, 1300 and 1800 numbers can all be ported to Click2Call for $100 inc GST each. Local and mobile numbers usually move in 5 to 10 business days; 1300 and 1800 numbers can take up to 30 days. Your number keeps working with your current provider the whole time and switches over on the day it moves.

The usual pattern is to test the system on a new number first, then lodge the port once you are happy. Do not cancel the old service before the port completes, or the number can be lost. The number porting page covers the details carrier by carrier.

What the AI adds to a business number

Three AI layers come with every Click2Call number at no extra charge, and each does one specific job. The AI Receptionist answers in your business name, asks what the caller needs and puts them through to the right person by voice, so a two-person business can offer a menu without recording one. The Answer Agent rings you first, and if you cannot pick up it takes the caller's name, number and reason, can book a time, and emails you a summary with the transcript. The AI Agent, for businesses that want it, holds a full conversation from a profile you write.

None of this replaces a person who wants to speak to you. It makes sure the call that arrives while you are driving, on a ladder or with another customer still ends up as a message in your inbox with the caller's details, rather than a missed call. The AI runs in Australia and the recordings and transcripts stay in Australia and New Zealand. The AI Receptionist page explains the three layers in detail.
