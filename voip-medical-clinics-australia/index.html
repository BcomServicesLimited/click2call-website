What happens at 8:30 on a Monday

Every practice knows the pattern. The doors open, the first patients arrive, and the phones light up with people wanting a same-day appointment, a repeat script, test results or a referral. Each receptionist can only take one call at a time, and anyone who rings during the rush hears an engaged tone or a phone that rings out.

Callers who get an engaged tone don't stop needing you. They redial, again and again, which stretches the rush out and frays tempers at the front desk. Some give up and book somewhere else.

A call queue changes that experience. Instead of an engaged tone, callers are answered by the system, hear your hold music and announcements, and are told where they are in the line. When a receptionist frees up, the caller who has waited longest is put through first.

  • ✓Announcements while patients wait, for example how to request repeat scripts online if your practice offers that
  • ✓Position in the queue, so callers know they're getting closer
  • ✓A schedule, so the queue only runs during opening hours
  • ✓The live Call Dashboard shows how many people are waiting, so the practice manager can pull another person onto the phones

The dashboard also counts the day's missed calls. Many practices have never measured that figure, and it's often the most revealing number in the building.

Set up the phones the way your front desk actually works

No two practices answer the phone the same way, so the system is made of simple building blocks that you arrange yourself, or that we arrange for you.

Ring groups

Every reception phone rings together and whoever is free picks up. Ideal for a small front desk.

Hunt groups

Calls try one person, then the next, in the order you set, so your most experienced receptionist gets first go.

Call queues

When everyone is busy, callers wait in line with music and announcements rather than hearing an engaged tone.

Direct lines

The practice manager, nurses or billing can have their own numbers, so not every call has to go through the front desk.

Extensions for consult rooms

Doctors and nurses transfer calls by extension number, from a desk phone or a softphone on the consult-room computer.

Missed-call alerts

Any call that rings out sends an email or SMS to whoever handles callbacks that day.

Start every call with the emergency message

A clinic line will sometimes be rung by someone who needs an ambulance, not an appointment. The first thing callers hear should tell them what to do: if this is a medical emergency, hang up and call triple zero (000).

With Click2Call you record that message once, or type it and let AI Speech read it in a natural voice, and it plays at the top of your greeting before any menu, queue or message option.

It's also why we're precise about what the AI tools do. The Answer Agent takes a message: it asks for the caller's name, number and reason for calling, waits for each answer, and emails the details with a transcript. The AI Receptionist routes calls when callers say who or what they need, such as 'appointments' or 'billing'. Neither one gives medical advice, assesses symptoms or decides how urgent a call is. That judgement stays with your clinical team.

Use the AI tools to stop calls going unanswered, not to make clinical decisions. Callbacks about symptoms, results or medication should always come from your staff.

After hours, weekends and public holidays

Each number on your account can follow business hours. When the practice closes, calls switch automatically to whatever you've set for after hours, and they switch back in the morning without anyone touching a button.

Common after-hours setups for clinics:

  • ✓Forward calls to your medical deputising service or after-hours GP service, using the number they give you
  • ✓Send calls to the on-call doctor's mobile, if your practice runs its own roster
  • ✓Play a message with your opening hours and after-hours options, then offer the Answer Agent for non-urgent messages

Public holidays are blocked out by date ahead of time, so Christmas, Easter and the Anzac Day long weekend don't need a manual change the night before.

If the clinic loses internet or power during the day, Forward when Offline sends calls to a mobile or another site. That works because the phone system runs in the cloud rather than in a box in your comms cupboard.

Recording, privacy and where your data is kept

Patients share sensitive information over the phone, so where call data lives matters. Click2Call stores call recordings, transcripts and call records in Australia and New Zealand.

Recording is off until you switch it on, and it's set per number or extension. That lets you record the main reception line, for training and resolving complaints, while leaving a doctor's direct line unrecorded. Where you do record, a notice can play to the caller first.

Portal access is controlled with individual logins, and two-factor login adds a code from a phone app when staff sign in. Give each person the access they need and no more.

Recording and privacy obligations vary between states and depend on your practice's own policies. Tell callers when calls are recorded, and make sure your privacy policy explains how recordings are used and stored.

Texts, recalls and fax

You can send SMS from your clinic's number from the portal, by email, or from your own software through the API. That suits appointment reminders and recall notices. Replies come back to an email inbox you choose and can be forwarded to a mobile. One text holds 160 characters, so keep reminders short.

Plenty of referrals still arrive by fax. Faxmail replaces the fax machine: faxes sent to your fax number arrive as email attachments, and you send one by emailing a document. A fax number is $10 a month, like any other line, and your existing fax number can be ported across.

There's no ready-made connection to practice management software. If you want call events in another system, the API and webhooks make it possible, but someone, typically your IT provider, needs to build the link.

Dental and allied health practices

Dental practices get a sharper version of the morning rush: patients with toothache ring first thing hoping for an emergency slot. A queue keeps them on the line, and an option in your greeting can send dental emergencies straight to whoever manages the day's book.

Physiotherapists, psychologists, podiatrists and other allied health practitioners often work without a full-time receptionist. When you're with a client for 50 minutes, the phone can't be answered. The Answer Agent rings your phone first and, if you're in a session, takes a message with the caller's details and reason for calling, so you can ring back between appointments.

Practitioners who visit patients at home or work across several clinics can carry the practice number on their mobile with the Secure VoIP app, so outbound calls show the clinic rather than their personal phone.

Clinics with more than one site

Group practices can keep each site's local number and add one number for the whole group. A menu or the AI Receptionist sends callers to the site they ask for, and an overflow rule can pass calls to another site's reception when the first is busy.

If you choose a 1300 number for the group, it costs $10 a month as a receive-only number, and incoming calls to it are charged at 20c a minute. Local numbers include unlimited incoming calls.

Every account starts with two call channels, meaning two calls at once, plus another channel for each user's number. A busy clinic can add more in the portal, so make sure your channel count keeps up with the number of people answering.
